Who is buying in the channel

Deals involving MSPs, MSSPs, VARs, ISVs and the platforms that buy them. Read from the acquirers’ own announcements, not from a spreadsheet anyone has to remember to update. Every row carries its source.

Active buyers

The question an owner actually has is not which deals happened, but who buys businesses like mine, and are they still buying. Sorted by how active they have been this year, then by their most recent deal.

No ranking, no recommendation. Facts and sources only.

Deal counts are of what this index holds, not of everything that happened. A buyer that announces quietly will be under-counted until another source catches it.

Deals

Newest first. A deal appears once a primary source names it.

Nothing matches those filters.

Why there is no price column. Price is disclosed in only a small minority of channel deals, and valuation almost never. Rather than print a column that is mostly blank, this index prints a price only inside the row where the acquirer actually stated one.

Where this comes from

  • ✓The acquirer’s own newsroom Serial buyers announce every tuck-in themselves. One platform’s own deal page carried 25 transactions.
  • ✓SEC filings Form 8-K item 2.01 for public acquirers — dated and not arguable.
  • ✓Advisor tombstones and fund portfolios Closed-deal pages at the firms that broker them, and add-on lists at the funds behind the buyers.
  • ✓ChannelE2E’s own reporting Structured here for the first time, as one source among several rather than the only one.

Scale check: an independent count put 2025 at 169 channel deals, 69% of them PE-backed (Omdia OM142467, 27 March 2026). That is roughly three a week — more than any newsroom can track by hand, which is the whole reason this runs on a schedule.

Dates carry the same three states as the events calendar: confirmed, derived, or held back until a human checks them. A publish date on a press page is not the deal date, and is treated as a signal to verify, not a fact.
